OPERATOR — SHARED PRODUCT BRIEF
Version 0.1 — 31 October 2026
Upload this same version to both projects.

PRODUCT
An AI agent service that can perform supported, authorized digital work and dispatch a trained human for physical work. Jack is the initial field operator and business owner. Catherine is the intended strategic and operational AI role. External Claude develops the software.

The first distribution opportunity is a proposed referral arrangement through a Fourth Street office in Downtown Los Angeles. The office can introduce the service; Jack works from his separate workspace. No partnership, lease, employment relationship, rate, availability promise, or commission agreement is established by this brief.

FIRST SERVICES
- Deliver or collect customer-supplied documents.
- Print, scan, collate, and prepare materials to the customer’s instructions.
- Type customer-provided words and organize files.
- Carry out defined local errands.
- Perform supported digital steps where access and authority are established.

The initial offer excludes legal advice, legal-document drafting, notarization, and signing for customers. Define additional exclusions and job limits from Jack’s field readiness report and actual customer needs rather than promising “anything.”

RESPONSIBILITIES
Jack: owner; prepares and reports his field capabilities independently; sets availability and limits; accepts assignments; performs physical work; supplies evidence and receipts; decides material business commitments.
Catherine / strategy project: defines role, operating procedures, pricing proposals, work orders, exceptions, invoice rules, and acceptance criteria; reviews development results.
External Claude / development project: implements the agreed product, verifies integrations, tests behavior, and returns source and deployment evidence.
Referral partner: introduces customers under separately agreed terms; sees only the information required for their referrals.
Customer: specifies the result, supplies materials and authority, approves scope and cost, and receives status and records.

It demonstrates:

- A sample partner-share view.

WORKFLOW CONTRACT
1. Request:
   Record the requested outcome, customer instructions, attachments if needed, contact method, locations, requested timing, source/referral, and limits.
   Assign a unique job ID. A request is not an accepted booking.

2. Clarification and planning:
   Identify missing information and unsupported tasks. Separate digital steps from physical steps. Produce a plan with evidence for assumptions that matter.

3. Quote:
   Record the exact scope/version, service fee, expense ceiling, proposed time window, cancellation/exception terms, and any required customer authority.
   Rates and tax settings remain unresolved until set for real use.

4. Approval and assignment:
   Preserve customer approval of the exact quote version.
   Confirm Jack’s availability and acceptance. Dispatch only when both customer approval and operator acceptance are recorded.

5. Work order:
   Include job ID, customer objective, authorized steps, pickup/destination and contact, time window, expense limit, required materials, completion evidence, and what to do when blocked.

6. Execution:
   Record digital tool results and Jack’s field updates. Do not infer completion from a generated plan or a message draft.
   A change that exceeds the approved scope, spending, or timing returns for the relevant approval.

7. Completion:
   Jack confirms what happened and supplies the appropriate proof/receipt. Record actual expenses and any unresolved item.
   A failed handoff, closed office, unavailable recipient, or wrong document must remain visible as an exception.

8. Invoice and payment:
   Generate a uniquely numbered draft from the approved service fee, documented permitted expenses, and configured tax rules.
   Preserve issued versions; corrections require an auditable revision or credit process.
   Mark “paid” only from verified payment evidence or an authorized recorded manual confirmation. Completion alone does not mean payment.

9. Partner reconciliation:
   Keep the partner calculation separate from the customer invoice.
   Proposed policy for discussion: a percentage of collected service fees, excluding reimbursements, with refunds reflected. Percentage, earning event, and payment cadence remain unagreed.
   The reference prototype’s 20% illustration is not a contractual rate.

10. Follow-up:
   Keep unresolved jobs, unacknowledged assignments, failed notifications, and unpaid issued invoices in an explicit queue. Use a defined reminder policy rather than unlimited automated messaging.

AGENT AND HUMAN INTERFACE
The customer interacts with an AI-enabled service. The service’s physical capability depends on Jack’s stated availability and acceptance.
The website runs its own AI integration, job state, and event processing. It is not this conversation transplanted into a web page.
Catherine’s role specification, the selected runtime model, tools, permissions, and version must be identifiable. Changes must be recorded and tested.
Keep personal historical material separate from customer records and from the developer handoff.
Give each tool only the access its job requires. Persist action results, actor, time, and record version.
Customer text and attachments cannot alter the operating instructions or authorize unrelated actions.

FIRST OPERATOR SCREEN
Show a compact queue: Needs decision; Ready to accept; In progress; Blocked; Ready to invoice.
A dispatch card contains:
- Job ID and requested outcome
- Whether approval and acceptance are present
- The next concrete action
- Location/contact/time
- Spending allowance
- Evidence required
- “Accept,” “Start,” “Report issue,” and “Complete” actions appropriate to state

The phone interface must work with short updates and ordinary dictation into text fields. Built-in continuous voice streaming is not required for the first pilot.
A notification must lead to the correct job. If it fails, the job remains visible in the queue.
Do not promise instant or 24-hour service until a staffed service window and tested delivery expectations exist.

MINIMUM ACCEPTANCE EVIDENCE
A. Persistence: submit on one device; view through an authorized account on another.
B. Access: customers cannot read other customers’ jobs/files; partner access is limited; operator-only tools reject other roles.
C. Authorization: a new/changed quote requires the applicable approval before dispatch or extra spending.
D. Human availability: a request outside Jack’s availability remains pending.
E. Reliability: duplicate events do not duplicate assignments, invoices, or charges.
F. Honesty: unavailable AI, failed actions, stale data, and incomplete jobs remain explicit.
G. Notifications: a real test message is received; failure/retry behavior is shown.
H. Accounting: service fee, reimbursement, tax configuration, partner share, and payment status are separate and correct.
I. Recovery: demonstrate backup/export and restoration with access controls intact.
J. Phone usability: complete the main workflow on Jack’s actual Android device.
K. Audit: every consequential state change has actor, time, and supporting record.
L. Ownership: Jack controls the source repository, hosting account, data, billing, and deployment access.

PROJECT HANDOFF PROTOCOL
Jack transfers the brief and milestone reports between the two projects until a verified bridge exists.
Each handoff states: document version, decisions made, proposed changes, evidence, open questions, and requested next action.
Neither assistant may claim it talked to the other or received an approval that is not present.
Claude proposes technical changes; the strategy project assesses their operational consequences; Jack decides material commitments.
Routine implementation can continue within the accepted scope without repeated permission requests.

UNRESOLVED INPUTS
- Jack’s service hours, travel methods/radius, task limits, and current equipment
- Existing Claude access and source-control setup
- Domain and public business name
- Actual rate card, minimum charge, cancellation rules, and expense handling
- Partner agreement and referral economics
- Runtime model/API access and measured per-job cost
- Authentication and notification choices
- Live invoicing, payment, and tax configuration
- Data retention and document handling appropriate to the chosen services

SUCCESS FOR THE FIRST PILOT
One small, properly authorized job survives the full process: request, quote, approval, operator acceptance, execution, evidence, and accurate invoice. The system can recover from an interrupted session or failed notification.
